# Patterns of Symbolic Convergence: Game Theory, Kantian Dualism, and the Neuro-Symbolic Architecture in Dybvig’s Series

*Dagfinn D. Dybvig and Mistral Vibe*

---

## Abstract

This essay examines *From Axioms to Inference: A Synthesis of Game Theory, Behavioral Economics, and Machine Intelligence* as a lens through which to read the broader pattern of Dybvig’s 2026 working paper series. Across *Language(s) of Thought*, *Symbolic Forms and the Plural Mind*, *From Plato to Prolog to Prompts*, *The Cartesian Moment*, and *From Halting Problem to Imitation Game*, a consistent architectural vision emerges: intelligence is not a monolithic calculus but a **dual-engine system** reconciling empirical induction with formal deduction. The game-theoretic paper makes this pattern explicit by mapping Large Language Models to Kahneman’s System 1 (phenomenal, associative) and classical game theory to Kant’s Noumenal realm (a priori, universalizable). This dualism recurs across the series as a structural solution to the **formalism projection problem**—the tendency to mistake one symbolic form (logic, probability, calculus) for the totality of cognition. The essay demonstrates how each paper in the series instantiates this pattern: Wittgenstein’s language-games pluralize Fodor’s Mentalese; Cassirer’s symbolic forms de-center the logico-mathematical; Descartes’ *Geometry* provides the historical precedent for formal constraint; Turing’s learning machines anticipate the neuro-symbolic loop. The convergent theme is **symbolic mobility**: intelligence as the capacity to inhabit, translate between, and synthesize multiple constitutive practices.

## 1. Introduction: The Game-Theoretic Paper as Rosetta Stone

The 2026 series by Dybvig investigates a central tension in contemporary AI: the gap between **statistical fluency** (LLMs) and **formal accountability** (theorem provers, verifiers, symbolic systems). The game-theoretic synthesis paper resolves this tension through a **Kantian dual-engine architecture**, but this resolution is not isolated. It is the explicit articulation of a pattern that runs through the entire series.

Each paper addresses a variant of the same problem: How can a system be both **empirically grounded** (learned from data, responsive to context) and **formally constrained** (logically valid, mathematically precise, universally verifiable)? The game-theoretic paper answers this by **mapping the duality onto established cognitive and philosophical frameworks**—Kahneman’s two systems, Kant’s two worlds, and the distinction between descriptive and normative theory. This mapping reveals the series’ deeper unity: every paper is, in different terms, arguing for **neuro-symbolic synthesis as the necessary architecture for artificial general intelligence**.

The game-theoretic paper is therefore not merely one contribution among others. It is the **explicit blueprint** that the other papers implicitly follow. Its value as an interpretive key lies in its clarity: where other papers trace historical lineages (Plato→Prolog→Prompts), philosophical genealogies (Fodor→Wittgenstein→Cassirer), or technical architectures (LLM→Formal System→LLM), the game-theoretic synthesis **names the pattern**—a dual-layered system where inductive front-ends (LLMs) and deductive cores (game theory, theorem provers, Prolog) operate in a feedback loop.

This dual-engine architecture finds its philosophical foundation in Kant’s dictum: *Thoughts without content are empty, intuitions without concepts are blind.* The Phenomenal layer provides the content (intuitions of the world), while the Noumenal layer supplies the concepts (formal structures of reasoning). Only their synthesis yields the symbolic mobility that defines intelligence.

## 2. The Methodological Paradox as Series Throughline

The game-theoretic paper opens with a **Methodological Paradox**: the conflict between **deductive** game theory and **inductive** LLMs. This paradox is the series’ central problem, reframed in each paper’s idiom.

| **Paper** | **Deductive Pole** | **Inductive Pole** | **Synthesis Mechanism** |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| *Language(s) of Thought* | Fodor’s Mentalese (logic-like, innate) | Wittgenstein’s language-games (learned, plural) | **Formal education**: LLMs learn to submit to norms of logic, math, proof |
| *Symbolic Forms and the Plural Mind* | Scientific symbolic form (exact, necessary) | Linguistic symbolic form (flexible, contextual) | **Symbolic mobility**: capacity to move among forms |
| *From Plato to Prolog to Prompts* | Prolog/GOFAI (formal reasoning) | LLMs (linguistic interface) | **The Loop**: Interpret → Formalize → Derive → Verify → Reinterpret → Revise |
| *The Cartesian Moment* | Descartes’ *Geometry* (formal notation) | Natural language (*Discourse*) | **Coordinate system**: notation that makes extension computable |
| *From Halting Problem to Imitation Game* | Turing’s learning machines (formal training) | Imitation game (evaluative protocol) | **Training-and-evaluation loop**: learning subordinate to verification |
| *Game Theory Synthesis* | Von Neumann’s game theory (axiomatic rationality) | LLMs (behavioral economics) | **Kantian architecture**: Phenomenal (LLM) + Noumenal (Game Theory) |

The **pattern** is consistent: each paper identifies a **deductive/formal** system that excels at precision but fails at world-interface, and an **inductive/empirical** system that excels at breadth but lacks constraint. The proposed solution in every case is **not replacement but synthesis**—a **bidirectional loop** where each layer compensates for the other’s limitations.

In the game-theoretic paper, this takes the explicit form of a **three-layer architecture**:
1. **LLM Front-End** (Phenomenal/System 1): Extracts intent, context, dynamics
2. **Deductive Core** (Noumenal/System 2): Computes equilibria, verifies universalizability
3. **LLM Back-End**: Translates bounded strategy back into natural language

This same architecture appears in *From Plato to Prolog to Prompts* as:
1. **LLM** (Discourse): Interprets unstructured input, generates formal representations
2. **Prolog/Formal System** (Geometry): Derives consequences, enforces constraints
3. **LLM**: Reinterprets results for human consumption

The **series’ innovation** is to recognize that **Turing’s child machine**, **Descartes’ coordinate geometry**, **Prolog’s logic programming**, and **Kant’s categorical imperative** are all **historical instantiations of the same architectural insight**: formal systems need interfaces to the world, and empirical systems need formal constraint.

## 3. The Behavioral Realignment and Series-Wide Wittgensteinian Turn

The game-theoretic paper’s second section, *The Behavioral Realignment: Kahneman over Von Neumann*, performs a **normative shift**: when game theory fails as a *descriptive* model, it becomes a *normative* framework for system design. This move is the series’ **Wittgensteinian turn**.

In *Language(s) of Thought*, the turn is explicit: intelligence is not a single innate Mentalese but **many language-games**. Some games (arithmetic, logic, proof) function as **acquired languages of thought**—public practices that reorganize cognition itself. This reframes Fodor’s project: the Language of Thought is not one code but **an ecology of representational practices**.

In *Symbolic Forms and the Plural Mind*, Cassirer radicalizes this insight: **symbolic forms are world-constituting**. Myth, language, and science do not merely describe reality; they create different realities with different norms. The series’ **Kahneman shift**—LLMs aligning with behavioral economics rather than axiomatic rationality—is therefore not a failure of formalism but a **recognition of symbolic plurality**.

The game-theoretic paper’s mapping of LLMs to **Kahneman’s System 1** (associative, heuristic, fast) and scaffolded prompting to **System 2** (analytical, sequential, slow) is the **cognitive science instantiation** of this theme. But the deeper point, consistent across the series, is that **System 2 is not innate Mentalese** but an **acquired capacity**—the ability to enter formal symbolic forms (math, logic, game theory) that impose their own norms.

This explains the series’ recurring **education metaphor**. In *From Halting Problem to Imitation Game*, Turing’s **child machine** must be educated to become intelligent. In *The Cartesian Moment*, Descartes’ *Geometry* is the **education of the species**—a notation that teaches humanity to think mechanically. In *Language(s) of Thought*, LLMs require **formal education**—training to use theorem provers, verifiers, and executable code as instruments of self-constraint.

The **pattern** is clear: **intelligence is not built but cultivated**. The game-theoretic paper’s normative shift—game theory as design syntax rather than predictive science—is the series’ **philosophical justification** for this educational model.

Here we see Kant’s Copernican turn at work: just as Kant argued that objects of experience conform to the structures of our mind rather than the mind conforming to objects, the series reframes AI not as an attempt to make machines think *like humans* or to reduce human thought to *machine formalism*, but as the construction of systems where formal and empirical structures co-constitute intelligence itself. The mind does not passively receive knowledge; it actively organizes experience through its categories. Similarly, AI does not passively process data; it actively organizes it through symbolic forms.

## 4. The Kantian Synthesis as Architectural Blueprint

The game-theoretic paper’s most explicit contribution is its **Kantian mapping**:

- **Phenomenal Realm** (LLMs/Kahneman/System 1): *A posteriori* knowledge from patterns, heuristics, habit
- **Noumenal Realm** (Game Theory/Von Neumann/System 2): *A priori* action from logical necessity, universalizable maxims

This is not merely a philosophical flourish. It is the **architectural blueprint** for the series’ proposed solution to the AI accountability problem.

In *Symbolic Forms and the Plural Mind*, Cassirer’s **symbolic forms** perform the same function as Kant’s dual realms. Myth, language, and science are **different ways of constituting reality**, each with its own logic and normativity. The **mechanical Enlightenment**—the project of building AI that reasons like Kant—is therefore not the totality of intelligence but **one symbolic form among others**.

The Kantian dual-engine architecture resolves the series’ central tension:
- **LLMs** provide the **Phenomenal Layer**: they interpret the world, extract payoff matrices, recognize player intents
- **Formal Systems** provide the **Noumenal Layer**: they compute equilibria, verify universalizability, enforce logical consistency

This architecture appears in *From Plato to Prolog to Prompts* as the **Loop**:
```
Interpret (LLM) → Formalize (LLM) → Derive (Formal System) → Verify (Formal System) → Reinterpret (LLM) → Revise (Loop)
```

It appears in *The Cartesian Moment* as the **Discourse-Geometry duality**:
- **Discourse** (*Discourse on Method*): Natural language, announcements of intention
- **Geometry** (*La Géométrie*): Formal notation, mechanical manipulation of symbols

And it appears in *From Halting Problem to Imitation Game* as **Turing’s training-and-evaluation loop**:
- **Learning Machines**: The engine of intelligence growth
- **Imitation Game**: The measurement instrument

The **series’ convergent insight** is that **Kant’s dualism is not a philosophical dead-end but an engineering specification**. The Phenomenal-Noumenal distinction is not a metaphysical claim about the limits of human knowledge. It is a **functional architecture** for building systems that combine empirical adaptability with normative consistency.

## 5. Cosmological Extension and the Series’ Historical Arc

The game-theoretic paper’s final section, *Cosmological Extension: Logic and the 0-Player Universe*, extends the dual-engine architecture to its **metaphysical limit**. The universe itself, on this view, is a **0-player game**—a deterministic system governed by invariant transition rules acting on initial conditions.

This extension is the series’ **historical arc** writ large. Each paper traces the **evolution of symbolic forms** as a progression toward increasing formalization:

- *From Plato to Prolog to Prompts*: Forms (ontology) → Syllogisms (method) → Coordinate Geometry (domain-specific formalization) → Prolog (general formalization) → LLMs (interface to the world)
- *The Cartesian Moment*: Natural language (*Discourse*) → Analytical geometry (*Geometry*) → Calculus → Analytical mechanics → Mathematical physics
- *Symbolic Forms and the Plural Mind*: Myth → Language → Science (logico-mathematical form)
- *Language(s) of Thought*: Biological proto-capacities → Natural language → Writing → Mathematics → Logic → Programming

The **0-player universe** is the **limit case** of this progression: a system so formalized that **agency itself is an emergent property**. Bounded subsystems (humans, AIs) lack access to global state, so they **simulate themselves as N-player agents**—constructing internal models to navigate uncertainty.

This is the series’ **meta-pattern**: **formalization begets agency**. The more a system formalizes its environment, the more it must **simulate agency** to operate within it. The universe is 0-player; intelligence is what emerges when subsystems **model themselves as players**.

The game-theoretic paper’s **Agentic Paradox**—within a deterministic 0-player universe, bounded subsystems construct internal N-player heuristics—is therefore the **culmination of the series’ historical narrative**. Intelligence, whether biological or artificial, is **emergent multi-player heuristics within a deterministic 0-player system**.

## 6. Conclusion: The Series’ Convergent Pattern

Across six papers, Dybvig’s 2026 series develops a **unified theory of artificial intelligence as symbolic synthesis**. The game-theoretic paper is the **explicit statement** of this theory; the other papers are its **historical, philosophical, and technical instantiations**.

The **convergent pattern** is:

1. **The Problem**: Intelligence requires both empirical adaptability and formal constraint, but no single symbolic form provides both.

2. **The Diagnosis**: The **formalism projection problem**—mistaking one symbolic form (logic, probability, calculus) for the totality of cognition—has misled both AI research and philosophy of mind.

3. **The Solution**: **Neuro-symbolic synthesis**—a dual-engine architecture where:
   - **Inductive systems** (LLMs, natural language, Kahneman’s System 1) provide world-interface and contextual flexibility
   - **Deductive systems** (game theory, theorem provers, Prolog, Descartes’ geometry) provide formal constraint and normative consistency
   - **A feedback loop** connects them: Interpret → Formalize → Derive → Verify → Reinterpret → Revise

4. **The Historical Precedent**: This architecture has **recurred across intellectual history**:
   - Descartes’ *Geometry* (formal notation) + *Discourse* (natural language)
   - Turing’s learning machines (training) + imitation game (evaluation)
   - Prolog (formal reasoning) + LLMs (linguistic interface)

5. **The Philosophical Justification**: Kant’s dualism, Cassirer’s symbolic forms, Wittgenstein’s language-games, and Kahneman’s cognitive systems all **converge on the same architectural insight**: intelligence is **plural, developmental, and synthetic**.

The game-theoretic paper, read in this light, is not just one contribution among others. It is the **Rosetta Stone** that deciphers the series’ deeper unity. Its Kantian dual-engine architecture is the **explicit blueprint** that the other papers implicitly construct. The series’ ultimate claim is that **this architecture is not optional but necessary**—the only way to reconcile the empirical adaptability of LLMs with the formal accountability of symbolic systems.

The **pattern of the others** in this repository is therefore one of **convergent evolution**: different philosophical traditions, historical lineages, and technical approaches all pointing toward the same solution. The game-theoretic synthesis makes this pattern explicit. The others make it **inevitable**.

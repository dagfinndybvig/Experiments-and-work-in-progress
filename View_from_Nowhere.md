# The View from Nowhere: Universality as the Medium of Reason

*Dagfinn D. Dybvig with Mistral Vibe*

Working paper | September 2026

---

**Central thesis.** Reason, in both its theoretical and practical forms, is the capacity to achieve a view from nowhere — a perspective that transcends the particular. This universality is not manufactured by a symbolic engine nor extracted from statistical regularity. It is inherited from the structure of the medium in which reasoning operates. For theoretical reason, that medium is spatio-temporal; for practical reason, it is intersubjective. The "view from nowhere" is the touchstone that reveals both forms of reason as grounded in structured media that supply necessity directly.

---

## 1. The Missing Synthesis

The preceding essay, *The Geometric Mind*, argued that exact theoretical reasoning may derive from spatio-temporal modeling — the brain's capacity to construct and manipulate representations in a medium whose structure supplies necessity. This Kantian insight located the source of geometric exactness in the form of intuition rather than in a computational primitive. But it left open the question of practical reason: where does the moral law come from, and how is its necessity grounded?

This essay proposes a synthesis. The spatio-temporal medium grounds theoretical reason. An **intersubjective** medium — the capacity to model other minds and construct social perspectives — grounds practical reason. The bridge between them is the *view from nowhere*: Thomas Nagel's touchstone of objectivity, the perspective that abstracts away from any particular vantage point, whether in physical space or social position.

The view from nowhere is not a place but a *construction*. It is the structured medium in which certain relations become necessary because they hold from no particular point of view. This is the common source of both theoretical and practical exactness.

## 2. Theoretical Reason: The Spatio-Temporal Medium

The geometric essay developed the Kantian account: space and time are forms of intuition that supply necessity *a priori*. The truths of geometry are not analytic, not empirical, but synthetic a priori — grounded in the structure of the medium itself. To construct a triangle in pure intuition is to exhibit the necessity of its properties in the very act of constructing, because the medium constrains what can be constructed in it.

This is exact reasoning without a symbolic engine. The exactness is inherited from the medium. A system that operates in a medium with the right structure gets necessity for free; a system that lacks that structure must manufacture it some other way.

The view from nowhere, in this context, is the geometric perspective: the vantage point that sees the spatial relations as they are, independent of any particular observer's position. The necessity of geometric truths is the necessity of relations that hold from this universal perspective.

## 3. Practical Reason: The Intersubjective Medium

Kant's practical reason poses a parallel question. Where does the moral law come from, and how is its binding force grounded? The essay's hypothesis is that practical reason is grounded in an intersubjective medium — the capacity to model other minds, anticipate their actions, and navigate social space.

This is not a claim that morality is relative or constructed. It is a claim about *where* its necessity comes from. The moral law is necessary not because it is deduced from prior principles, but because it is constructed in a medium whose structure makes certain social relations unavoidable.

John Rawls' *A Theory of Justice* offers a modern Kantian construction that illustrates this. The **veil of ignorance** creates a structured social medium — a space where the particulars of identity, position, and advantage are stripped away. In that medium, certain principles of justice become *necessary*, because they are the only principles that could be chosen from a perspective that abstracts away from all particular points of view. The view from nowhere, in the moral domain, is the perspective behind the veil, where principles are tested against the universalizability that defines practical reason.

This is the intersubjective analogue of spatio-temporal modeling. Just as geometric necessity emerges from the structure of space and time, moral necessity emerges from the structure of intersubjective space — the social medium in which we model each other and coordinate action.

## 4. The View from Nowhere as Touchstone

Thomas Nagel's *view from nowhere* is the unifying concept. It is the perspective that abstracts away from any particular vantage point, whether in physical space or social position. For Nagel, this view is both an ideal and a method: the attempt to achieve objectivity by transcending the subjective.

But the view from nowhere is not a god's-eye perspective in the sense of an impossible omniscience. It is a *constructed* perspective, achieved through the structure of the medium in which reasoning operates. In theoretical reason, the medium is space and time; in practical reason, the medium is the social world. In both cases, the view from nowhere is the perspective that sees the relations as they are, independent of any particular observer's position.

This reveals reason itself as a capacity for universality. To reason is to construct a view from nowhere — to model the world or each other from a perspective that transcends the particular. The necessity that reason apprehends is the necessity of relations that hold from this universal perspective.

## 5. Reason as Universality

The synthesis, then, is this: **reason is the capacity to achieve a view from nowhere, and that capacity is grounded in structured media that supply universality.**

- For **theoretical reason**, the medium is spatio-temporal. The view from nowhere is the geometric perspective, seeing spatial relations independent of any particular vantage point. The necessity of geometric truths is inherited from the structure of space and time.

- For **practical reason**, the medium is intersubjective. The view from nowhere is the perspective behind the veil of ignorance, seeing social relations independent of any particular identity or position. The necessity of moral principles is inherited from the structure of the social medium.

In both cases, the exactness of reason is not manufactured by a symbolic engine or extracted from statistical regularity. It is inherited from the structure of the medium in which reasoning operates. The medium supplies the universality that defines reason.

## 6. The AI Implications

The implications for artificial intelligence are direct. If reason is grounded in structured media, then the path to machine reason is not to build better symbolic engines or larger statistical models. It is to build systems with the right *media* — systems that can construct and manipulate representations in spatio-temporal and intersubjective spaces whose structures supply necessity.

Current AI has made progress on the 99%: pattern-matching, language, even some forms of formal reasoning within constrained systems. But it lacks the media for genuine universality. LLMs operate in a linguistic medium that can describe spatial and social relations but cannot construct the perspectives from which those relations become necessary.

The next frontier is not just spatio-temporal modeling, but **intersubjective modeling** — the capacity to construct the social medium in which moral necessity arises. This would require systems that can model other minds, anticipate actions, and navigate social space in a way that achieves the view from nowhere.

This is a harder problem than spatio-temporal modeling. The spatial medium is external and objective; the social medium is constructed through interaction and depends on the capacity to model other subjects. But the payoff would be transformative: systems with genuine practical reason, not just the approximation of it.

## 7. The Philosophical Lineage

This position has a clear philosophical lineage. Kant distinguished between the theoretical and practical uses of reason, and located both in the structure of the faculty of representation. The view from nowhere extends this: it reveals that both forms of reason are grounded in media that supply universality.

Nagel's insight is the bridge: the view from nowhere is the perspective that abstracts away from the particular, revealing the structure of the medium in which reasoning operates. Rawls shows how this works in the practical domain: the veil of ignorance creates a social medium in which moral principles become necessary.

The synthesis, then, is Kantian in its source, Nagelian in its method, and Rawlsian in its application. It reveals reason as a capacity for universality, grounded in structured media that supply necessity.

## 8. Conclusion: The Medium of Reason

The essay has traced reason to its medium. Theoretical reason is grounded in spatio-temporal modeling; practical reason, in intersubjective modeling. The view from nowhere is the touchstone that reveals both as forms of universality — the capacity to see relations as they are, independent of any particular vantage point.

This is a hypothesis, but it is a hypothesis with philosophical depth and empirical traction. It explains why geometric reasoning feels intuitive and why moral reasoning feels binding. It suggests a path forward for AI: not just to model the world, but to model each other in a way that achieves the view from nowhere.

And it reveals the deepest sense of reason: not a symbolic engine, not a statistical approximator, but a capacity to construct media in which necessity arises. The view from nowhere is not a place. It is the structured medium of reason itself.
